Homework 7: Spanning Trees and Greedy Algorithms

The first minimum spanning tree algorithm was Otakar Borůvka’s in 1926, for laying out an electrical network in Moravia. Vojtěch Jarník found the algorithm now called Prim’s in 1930, 27 years before Prim. R. L. Graham and P. Hell, “On the History of the Minimum Spanning Tree Problem,” Annals of the History of Computing 7(1), 1985

Six functions: the two union-find operations (find with path compression, unite by size), Kruskal’s and Prim’s minimum spanning tree algorithms, activity selection by earliest finish time, and Huffman coding with a stated tie-breaking rule. The starter has a self-running test battery; you fill in six TODOs. Quiz 6 (Monday Nov 23) covers this material too.

Tasks

TODOFunctionWhat it must do
1findReturn the root of an element’s tree, compressing the path on the way (path halving or full compression).
2uniteMerge two sets by size, the smaller root under the larger; on a tie the first argument’s root survives.
3kruskalThe edges of a minimum spanning forest, in the order Kruskal accepts them, with your union-find as the cycle test.
4primThe weight of a minimum spanning tree of the start vertex’s component, by lazy Prim with a priority queue.
5maxActivitiesThe most activities one room can hold, chosen by earliest finish time, with half-open intervals.
6huffmanCodesThe codeword of every symbol in the Huffman tree built by the tie-breaking rule in the contract.

The full contract of each function, with examples, is in the comment above it in the starter. Every function is also tested on large inputs (a million elements, or graphs with 100,000 vertices and 1,000,000 edges) with a time limit: write each one with the running time its contract states.
