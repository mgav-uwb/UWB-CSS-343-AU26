Homework 4: Heaps and Balanced-Tree Invariants

CSS 343 · Week 4 · Lecture 6 (Balanced Trees II) and Lecture 7 (Heaps)
At a glance
Format: individual Language: C++17 Starter: hw04.cpp Submit: a .zip with your hw04.cpp Due: Sun Oct 25, 11:59 PM

Six functions: two on heaps with d children per node instead of two, two that use a priority queue to solve a problem, one that checks the left-leaning red-black invariants, and one that counts the keys in the sparsest possible B-tree. The starter has a self-running test battery; you fill in six TODOs. Quiz 4 (Monday Oct 26) asks about the same material.

Tasks

TODOFunctionWhat it must do
1sinkDarySink one element in a 0-based d-ary max-heap, swapping with the largest child (the leftmost on ties).
2buildDaryTurn an array into a d-ary max-heap in linear time, sinking the internal nodes from the last to the root.
3kthLargestThe k-th largest value, with a min-heap of at most k values and no sorting.
4mergeSortedMerge k sorted lists into one, with a min-heap holding one entry per list.
5llrbBlackHeightCheck the three left-leaning red-black invariants and return the black height, or −1.
6minKeysBTreeThe fewest keys a B-tree of order M and height h can hold.

The full contract of each function, with examples, is in the comment above it in the starter. For TODOs 3 and 4 you may use std::priority_queue; the starter shows how to declare a min-heap.

The tests run themselves

g++ -std=c++17 -O2 hw04.cpp -o hw04 && ./hw04

Each check prints [PASS] or [FAIL]. The unmodified starter compiles and fails most of them.
