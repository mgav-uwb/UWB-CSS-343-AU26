Homework 5: Hash Tables and Graph Search

“Data dominates. If you've chosen the right data structures and organized things well, the algorithms will almost always be self-evident.” Rob Pike, “Notes on Programming in C,” 1989
At a glance
Covers: Lecture 8 (Hashing) and Lecture 9 (Graphs I) Format: individual Language: C++17 Starter: hw05.cpp Submit: a .zip with your hw05.cpp Due: Sun Nov 1, 11:59 PM

Six functions: three on hash tables (an insert by double hashing, a delete for linear probing that keeps every probe path intact, and an insert for separate chaining that resizes), and three on graph search (component sizes, the fewest moves through a maze, and a shortest path by number of edges). The starter has a self-running test battery; you fill in six TODOs. Quiz 5 (Monday Nov 2) asks about the same material.

Tasks

TODOFunctionWhat it must do
1dhInsertInsert a key by double hashing into a fixed-size table, returning its slot, or −1 when every probe fails.
2lpRemoveRemove a key from a linear-probing table and re-insert the rest of its cluster, so every remaining key is still found.
3chainInsertInsert into a separate-chaining table, resizing to 2M + 1 and rehashing when the load factor passes 2.
4componentSizesThe size of every connected component of an undirected graph, in order of each component's smallest vertex.
5gridStepsThe fewest moves from S to E in a grid maze with walls, or −1.
6bfsPathA path with the fewest edges from s to t, as its list of vertices.

The full contract of each function, with examples, is in the comment above it in the starter. TODOs 4 and 5 are also tested on graphs and grids with hundreds of thousands of vertices.

The tests run themselves

g++ -std=c++17 -O2 hw05.cpp -o hw05 && ./hw05

Each check prints [PASS] or [FAIL]. The unmodified starter compiles and fails most of them.
